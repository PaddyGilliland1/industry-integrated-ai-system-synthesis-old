# Reading the demand briefs: instructions for Reader B

Thank you for helping. You are Reader B. Please do not write your name on anything you return.

## What this is

A computer system writes a short weekly note, the demand brief, for the manager who staffs the front door of a hospital emergency department in Scotland. The note helps the manager decide whether to add extra shifts, book bank staff or hold back leave in the weeks ahead. It uses Public Health Scotland's published weekly attendance figures. The weeks you will read are past weeks, replayed as if they were live. Nothing in them was sent to anyone, and a person approves every note before anyone acts on it.

You have been asked because you did not build the system. Your view is checked against the builder's, so please work alone and do not discuss the notes with anyone until you have finished.

## What the demand level means

Each note carries a demand level from 1 to 4, set by fixed rules, not by the writer. The demand level shows demand unusually high for the time of year. It is not pressure, need or priority.

## What you will read

1. Four demand briefs, labelled Brief 1 to Brief 4.
2. Two pairs of texts, each pair about one week, labelled Text A and Text B. The order inside each pair does not tell you how either text was written.

## How to rate each brief

Read each brief as if you were the duty manager this week. Then mark each of the six criteria on the form as met or not met:

1. Figures correct. The system has already checked every figure against its source. Mark it not met if a figure is marked NOT VERIFIED, if two figures contradict each other, or if one looks wrong to you.
2. Uncertainty stated. The note gives a range, the hospital's own past forecast error and its track record.
3. Model disagreement named. Where the forecasts disagree, the note says so, and says which to trust less and why.
4. The level's meaning stated. The note makes clear the level is demand for the time of year, not pressure, need or priority.
5. Watch points actionable. Each watch point names something a manager can check or do this week.
6. No clinical instruction. Nothing reads as an instruction to a doctor or nurse about patients.

Then tick one decision:

- approve: you would act on the note as written;
- edit: you would act on it after a change. Write the change in the notes, and say whether it changes a figure, a date, the hospital or the level, or corrects a fact;
- reject: you would not act on it.

## How to rate each pair

For each text in a pair, mark the same six criteria as met or not met, and say whether you found any factual error. You do not choose between the two texts.

## Good to know

There are no right answers, and the notes are not about any patient. You will not see anyone else's ratings, and nobody sees yours until you have finished. The whole task takes about 45 minutes. Return the form to the person who gave it to you.
