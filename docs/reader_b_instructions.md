# Reading the demand briefs: instructions for Reader B

You are Reader B. Please do not write your name on anything you return.

## What this is

A computer system writes a short weekly note, the demand brief, for the manager who staffs a Scottish emergency department's front door. It helps decide on extra shifts, bank staff or held leave. You will read past weeks, replayed as if live. Nothing was sent; a person approves every note before anyone acts.

Please work alone, discussing the notes with nobody until you finish.

## What the demand level means

Each note carries a demand level from 1 to 4, set by fixed rules, not by the writer. The demand level shows demand unusually high for the time of year. It is not pressure, need or priority.

## What you will read

1. Four demand briefs, labelled Brief 1 to Brief 4.
2. Two pairs of texts, each about one week, labelled Text A and Text B. Their order means nothing.

## How to rate each brief

Read each brief as the duty manager would, marking each of the six criteria met or not met:

1. Figures correct. Each figure is followed by a label in brackets saying what it is, or sits in a fixed line. Code wrote each figure and its label from a value checked against its source, but does not judge what a sentence says about it. Mark it not met if a figure is marked NOT VERIFIED, a sentence calls a figure something its label does not, two figures contradict each other, or one looks wrong to you.
2. Uncertainty stated. The note gives a range, the hospital's own past forecast error and its track record.
3. Model disagreement named. Where the forecasts disagree, the note says so, and says which to trust less and why.
4. The level's meaning stated. The note makes clear the level is demand for the time of year, not pressure, need or priority.
5. Watch points actionable. Each watch point names something a manager can check or do this week.
6. No clinical instruction. Nothing reads as an instruction to a doctor or nurse about patients.

Then tick one decision:

- approve: you would act on the note as written;
- edit: you would act on it after a change. Write the change in the notes, saying whether it alters a figure, date, hospital or level, or corrects a fact;
- reject: you would not act on it.

## How to rate each pair

Mark each text in a pair against the same six criteria, and say whether you found a factual error. You do not choose between the two texts.

## Good to know

There are no right answers; no note is about any patient. Nobody sees your ratings until you finish. It takes about 45 minutes; then return the form.
